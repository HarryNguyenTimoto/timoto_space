# The TiMoto Knowledge Base: A Guide for Everyone

The TiMoto knowledge base is our shared handbook for how the company’s products and systems work. It lives in Mintlify and includes product journeys, team and system guides, frequently used procedures, and technical references. You do not need to be an engineer to find or improve useful information.

There are two easy ways to use it:

1. Open Mintlify and search or browse the pages yourself.
2. Ask Tom in Slack to find, explain, or prepare a draft update to the information.

<Note>
  Mintlify is the place people can read the published handbook. Tom is a shortcut for finding and understanding it. Tom’s current setup is read-only: he can search and read the knowledge base and help draft an update, but he cannot publish changes to Mintlify himself. A person with Mintlify editing access must review and publish the change.
</Note>

## 1. What belongs in the knowledge base?

Use the knowledge base when someone on the team should be able to find the same useful answer again later. Good topics include:

- What a customer, dealer, or operations teammate does in a product flow.
- What a feature is for and what should happen when it works.
- Where to find a product or service, and which team owns it.
- A recurring support or operations procedure.
- A decision the team made and the reason behind it.
- A simple explanation of how two parts of TiMoto work together.
- A link to the right product page, form, dashboard, or internal guide.

The knowledge base is not a place to put every conversation. Add the lasting answer, not the entire Slack discussion. If the information is temporary, private to one person, or likely to change tomorrow, keep it in the system that owns it instead.

The handbook is also not a live status dashboard. A page can describe how a system is intended to work without proving that it is currently running. For “Is this live right now?” or “Did this deployment succeed?”, check the current system or ask the responsible owner. If the current status has not been verified, say so.

## 2. Read the knowledge base directly in Mintlify

Open the private TiMoto docs site at [timoto-ai.mintlify.app](https://timoto-ai.mintlify.app) and sign in with your authorized company account. If you cannot open it, ask the documentation owner for access. Do not make the site public to work around a sign-in problem.

### Find a page by browsing

Use the navigation on the left when you know the general area:

- **Start here** introduces the handbook and how to use it.
- **Product** explains what the product does.
- **End-to-End Flows** describes journeys that cross more than one screen or team.
- **Architecture & Systems** explains how company systems fit together.
- **APIs & Postman** is a more technical area for people working with integrations.
- The product-specific sections hold detailed guides for mobile, dealer, operations, web, and data tools.

Open a section, choose a page, and use its headings to jump to the part you need. The title and introduction should tell you whether the page is for your question. Follow links to related pages when an answer spans several areas.

### Find a page with search

Use the Mintlify search box and try the words you would naturally use. You do not need to know a file name or a technical term. For example, try “dealer payment”, “reschedule pickup”, “how to price a bike”, or the exact error someone saw.

If the first search does not help:

1. Try a shorter phrase.
2. Try a product name or the name of the screen you saw.
3. Try a word from the message or page you are investigating.
4. Open the closest result and follow its related links.

Read the page’s date, status, and source notes if shown. If something appears old or conflicts with what you see in the product, do not guess which one is right—ask the page owner or the team responsible for that flow.

## 3. Ask Tom to find or explain something

Tom is available in Slack. Open a direct message with Tom, or mention **@Tom** in a channel where Tom has been invited. In a channel, include the mention so Tom knows you are asking him.

You can ask in normal language. Examples:

> @Tom, where is the guide to the dealer checkout flow? Give me the page name and the relevant section.

> @Tom, explain the pickup rescheduling process in simple terms. Tell me which Mintlify pages support your answer.

> @Tom, does the knowledge base say this feature is live, or does it only describe how it should work?

For a useful answer, include any detail you already know: the product area, who is doing the task, a screen or page name, the error wording, or what you are trying to accomplish. You can ask Tom to explain unfamiliar words or summarize a long page.

Tom searches the company knowledge base and reads relevant pages before answering. Ask him to include the page path and section or line numbers so you can open and check the source yourself. The mirror Tom searches refreshes from the Mintlify documentation source about once an hour, so Mintlify may show a recent change before Tom’s copy does. For the newest published version, open Mintlify directly.

If Tom cannot find an answer, ask him to tell you what he searched for and which nearby page might help. “I couldn’t find it in the current handbook” is better than a confident guess.

## 4. Add or correct information yourself in Mintlify

Before creating a new page, search for the subject. If a good page already exists, update that page instead of making a second copy. This helps the next person find one reliable answer.

### Prepare the update

Write down, in ordinary language:

- What changed or what someone needs to know.
- Who the information is for.
- When the change takes effect, if that matters.
- Where the information came from: for example, an approved product decision, a current process, or a page owner.
- Who can confirm it if something is uncertain.

Separate confirmed facts from plans and open questions. Do not turn “we might do this” into “this is how the product works.” If you do not know the answer to a detail, mark it as a question for the owner.

### Edit an existing page

1. Sign in to Mintlify with your company account and open the **timoto-ai** documentation site.
2. Find the page using the navigation or search.
3. Use the site’s editing experience to open the page for editing.
4. Make the smallest change that gives readers the correct answer. Keep the title and surrounding page structure unless they are also misleading.
5. Preview the result as a reader. Check that the new wording is clear and that nearby instructions still make sense.
6. Save the change for review using the publishing flow available to your account. Ask the owner of the process to confirm important product or policy changes.
7. After it is approved and published, reopen the page on the private docs site and confirm the published version looks right.

### Add a new page

Create a new page only when the information does not belong naturally on an existing page. Give it a short title that says what it answers. Put it in the section where a reader would look for it, and link it from a related page if that helps people find it. Start with the answer, then add the steps or context readers need.

A useful page answers these simple questions: What is this? Who is it for? What should I do? What should I expect? Who can help if it does not work?

## 5. Ask Tom to help prepare an update

Tom can make the work easier even when you do not know which page needs changing. Ask him to find the current page, point out related pages, and prepare clear replacement wording for a human to review.

For example:

> @Tom, the dealer checkout instructions changed on 28 September. Find the relevant Mintlify page. Draft a plain-language update using these confirmed facts: [write the facts]. Keep uncertain items as questions, and tell me where the draft should go.

Or:

> @Tom, this paragraph is out of date: [paste the paragraph]. Find the closest existing page and suggest a corrected version. Do not publish anything; show me the source pages and the proposed text.

Tom can search and read the current Mintlify mirror. In the current deployment, he cannot save pages, change the navigation, or publish to Mintlify. To finish the update, copy the reviewed draft into the Mintlify editor, preview it, and submit it through the site’s normal review and publishing flow. If Tom’s permissions change in the future, a direct-edit workflow should still show the proposed change for human review before it becomes published documentation.

## 6. Keep updates safe and trustworthy

Before publishing, check the page as if you were the next teammate trying to use it:

- Is the information clear without the Slack conversation that led to it?
- Does it say who the steps are for?
- Are facts, plans, and unknowns kept separate?
- Is the page in the right section, and can people find it by search?
- Does it link to the most helpful related guide?
- Has someone responsible for the flow reviewed important changes?

Never add passwords, sign-in codes, API keys, payment details, private customer information, or unedited screenshots of private conversations. Use a general example instead. If sensitive information appears in a draft by mistake, remove it before saving and tell the documentation owner.

If you find two pages that disagree, do not silently choose one. Ask the page owners which one is current, then correct or label the outdated page. If a page is missing, tell Tom or the docs owner what you searched for; that feedback can help the team add a page later.

## Quick guide

| What you need | Best next step |
| --- | --- |
| Read about a product or process | Search or browse the private Mintlify site |
| Get a short explanation with sources | Ask Tom in Slack and request the page path |
| Fix wording or add confirmed information | Edit in Mintlify and submit it for review |
| Need help finding the right page or wording | Ask Tom to locate the page and prepare a draft |
| Need current production status | Check the live system or ask its owner; do not rely on a handbook page alone |

The goal is simple: make useful company knowledge easy to find, easy to understand, and safe to keep current.
